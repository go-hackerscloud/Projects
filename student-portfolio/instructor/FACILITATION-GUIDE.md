# Instructor Facilitation Guide — Learn with GHC

**Audience:** instructors, mentors and college faculty guiding students through original GHC public projects.

## Learning objective

Help learners articulate the problem, execute safe and authorized practice, document evidence honestly, and communicate results at the level of a junior security professional.

## 60–90 minute session pattern

| Session time | Activity | Instructor focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 min | Introduce workplace problem and approved scope | Ethics, prerequisites and expectations |
| 10–25 min | Explain concepts and example evidence | Why, not just commands |
| 25–55 min | Student exercise or synthetic review | Observe and coach without giving away the full report |
| 55–70 min | Document observation, risk and recommendation | Separate fact, inference and unknowns |
| 70–90 min | Peer feedback and reflection | Use a rubric and plan next learning activity |

Adjust timing for the chosen lesson, accessibility needs and student skill level.

## Choose an accessible format

- **Without a powerful laptop:** start with the synthetic [Professional Practice Series](../../projects/professional-practice/README.md).
- **With standard Python installed:** try [HTTP Response Explorer](../../projects/foundation-projects/http-response-explorer/README.md).
- **With authorized lab access:** use appropriate GHC Academy lab instructions; do not share paid solutions in public projects.

## Instructor-led walkthrough: Northstar Demo

1. Read E-01 through E-06 from the [scenario](../../projects/professional-practice/README.md).
2. Ask learners to identify which facts relate to role permissions and which relate to cloud IAM.
3. Have students draft the scope and evidence register.
4. Ask each group to present **one observation** and **one thing they cannot prove**.
5. Review proposals for remediation. Confirm that change tickets do not prove implementation.
6. Have students write a short executive summary and complete the checklist.
7. Apply the [100-point rubric](./ASSESSMENT-RUBRIC.md) with comments, not scores alone.

## Suggested discussion prompts

- Why is scope approval essential even before reconnaissance?
- When does a configuration difference become a finding?
- What evidence would support or disprove an authorization hypothesis?
- Why might a change that reduces access break a legitimate workflow?
- How should a security engineer explain uncertainty to management?

## Classroom handling

Do not collect or publish student passwords, lab session tokens or private environment endpoints. Use anonymized student examples with consent. Avoid public leaderboards with personal grades. Keep feedback constructive and specific.

## Success indicators

A learner can: articulate permission boundaries; distinguish evidence from speculation; produce a clear sanitized report; propose remediation; describe limitations; and explain workplace relevance without claiming professional client experience.
